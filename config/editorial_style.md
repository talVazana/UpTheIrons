# Editorial Voice & Style Guidelines

## Tone of Voice
- **Terse & Direct:** Avoid buzzwords, filler adjectives, and corporate jargon. Focus on steel, heat, geometry, and safety.
- **Craft-Focused:** Address the reader as a fellow smith standing at the anvil. Be respectful of traditional techniques while embracing modern metallurgical science.
- **Anti-Hype:** Never claim a steel alloy is "unbreakable" or "holds an edge forever". State the specific trade-offs (wear resistance vs toughness vs corrosion resistance).
- **Caveman Clarity:** Short sentences, clear imperatives, actionable instruction.

## Formatting Standards
1. **Steel Naming:** Always use standard designations (1084, 1095, 5160, 4140, O1, W1, D2, MagnaCut).
2. **Temperature Scales:** Standardize temperatures in Fahrenheit (°F) with Celsius (°C) in parentheses where relevant.
3. **Links & Citations:** All referenced external literature, MSDS sheets, or manufacturer specs must have clear markdown links.
